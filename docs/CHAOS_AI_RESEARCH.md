# Adapting the single player AI to Chaos Chess

## Behavior before this change

- The multiplayer rule in [`game/chaos.py`](../game/chaos.py) starts each piece at 20 stamina. Pawn, knight, bishop, rook, and queen moves cost 1, 4, 4, 5, and 9. A piece changes color **once**, when its score crosses from positive to zero or below; a castling rook also pays its move cost. The function handles captures, en passant, and promotion, then returns FEN plus square-indexed stamina JSON.
- The single player path in [`game/consumers.py`](../game/consumers.py) accepts only PGN and level. [`game/chessAI.py`](../game/chessAI.py) rebuilds an ordinary `chess.Board` from PGN, searches ordinary `board.push()` moves, and tries `bookfish.bin` first. Its material evaluation and search therefore cannot predict defection. The browser in [`game/templates/game/single.html`](../game/templates/game/single.html) also runs standard `chess.js` rules and expects one UCI move in response.

## Recommended smallest design

1. Make a search position `(board, stamina)` and apply **the same transition as `game.chaos.apply_move` at every node**, including both human and AI turns. A concrete first implementation can call `apply_move(board.fen(), state, from_square, to_square, board.turn)` and construct each child from its returned FEN and state. A later optimization can extract one shared in-memory transition if profiling shows FEN/JSON conversion is too expensive. `python-chess` supplies legal moves and FEN reconstruction; `Board.push()` alone only applies ordinary chess rules. [Board API](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/stable/core.html#board).
2. Pass the **current FEN and stamina** to the AI, preferably from server-owned game state, rather than rebuilding from PGN. PGN replay applies recorded chess moves to a conventional board; it has no built-in record of this repo's stamina/color transition. [PGN node board API](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/stable/pgn.html), [PGN source](https://github.com/niklasf/python-chess/blob/master/chess/pgn.py).
3. Retain negamax/alpha-beta, but evaluate each *resulting Chaos position*: material after defection, ordinary positional terms, and a modest risk term for valuable pieces close to their next defection. Treat a move that defects a queen as a tactical event even if it is not a capture. The current capture-only quiescence misses these moves; extend its tactical set or use fixed-depth evaluation initially. Check terminal positions **before** stand-pat evaluation, and search legal evasions while in check. Stockfish's search explicitly enters quiescence at depth zero and handles check separately. [Stockfish search source](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/master/src/search.cpp).
4. Disable the Polyglot opening book in Chaos mode. Its position key includes pieces, turn, castling, and en passant, but no stamina; identical boards with different stamina have different futures. The same applies to any future transposition or repetition key: include normalized stamina for live pieces as well as the board position. [Polyglot hash source](https://github.com/niklasf/python-chess/blob/master/chess/polyglot.py).
5. Have the single player browser display and submit Chaos state and accept the resulting FEN/state after both moves. `chess.js` may still display pieces through FEN, but its ordinary move application cannot perform the color change. Let the server's `apply_move` decide move legality and post-move state. This follows the existing multiplayer path in [`game/consumers.py`](../game/consumers.py).

## Rule edges and verification

The implementation now uses server-owned FEN and stamina for each single-player WebSocket, a fixed-depth (1–3 ply) negamax/alpha-beta search, and a score that prioritizes checkmate, then material, positional value, and stamina. The standard chess mode retains its existing search and opening book. Focused tests cover mate choice, a queen that would defect, and a complete human/AI WebSocket exchange.

- **Defection and king safety:** The existing rule validates a move before switching the piece's color. A new enemy piece can therefore give check to the side that just moved. Single player preserves that rule so both modes match. `python-chess` documents that arbitrary board edits can produce invalid positions and recommends `is_valid()` to detect them. A future rules decision should settle whether this move remains legal. [Board validity docs](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/stable/core.html#board).
- **State and search history:** `Board.set_piece_at()` clears the move stack; the current transition returns FEN and is naturally a fresh search position. Built-in repetition checks rely on board history and do not account for stamina. Use an explicit `(position, stamina)` history if Chaos repetition is a rule. [Board mutation and repetition docs](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/stable/core.html#board).
- **Promotion:** `apply_move` always tries a queen promotion, while `board.legal_moves` also includes other promotion choices. Until the transition accepts a promotion piece, search only the queen promotion for a given pawn move so the AI does not score several distinct moves as the same position. [Legal move and promotion API](https://python-chess.readthedocs.io/en/stable/core.html#board).
- **Connection lifetime:** The single-player Chaos position lives on its WebSocket connection. A reconnect starts a new game; resuming across connections would require persisted game identity and state.
